## 1. What the evidence shows

Across the four flagship projects, the work Mikel led has a clear pattern:

| Project | Led by Mikel |
|---|---|
| Ardura | Development phase and CAD, final renders |
| SEAT mirror | Regulatory analysis, circuit redesign, material specifications manual, materials justification, final renders |
| Mercedes vent | Life-cycle analysis, improvement goals and ideas, environmental evaluation |
| Orbea rack | Market and usage analysis, ideation sketches, specifications document, manufacturing process, final renders |

He consistently owns the stage where a concept becomes a buildable, specified product (CAD, materials, regulations, circuits, manufacturing, environmental impact) and then presents the result (final renders). That is what a design engineer does. It also separates him from portfolios that show only form and renders.

**Positioning:** a product designer who takes concepts through engineering development and presents them convincingly.

Why it works: it is first person, specific, verifiable against the case studies, and it doesn't claim professional experience.

## 5. Tone of voice

- **Plain and specific.** "I redesigned the mirror's electrical circuit" instead of "I drove innovative solutions".
- **First person singular for his work, plural for the team's.** "We researched…, I led the CAD…" keeps it honest about team PBL work.
- **Engineering register, not marketing register.** Numbers only when the source report gives them.
- **Short.** Case-study paragraphs of 2–4 sentences. Images and diagrams carry the process.
- **No superlatives.** Avoid "passionate", "innovative", "cutting-edge", "seamless".

## 6. Content rules

1. Every case study opens with a **role block**: year, course, team (named), company collaboration (only in approved wording), **"My role"** as a short list of what Mikel led.
2. Collaborations are worded exactly as confirmed: e.g. "Academic project in collaboration with Orbea". Never "client work" or "for Orbea".
3. Recognition is stated precisely or not at all. Ardura: at most "Submitted to [exact iF competition name]". Ibili and UPV: only with evidence.
4. Data (LCA results, specs, prices) is quoted only as it appears in the source reports.
5. Teammates are credited by name on every case study.
6. Areas of interest (AI, robotics, automation, IoT) appear as **interests**, not skills, until a project supports them.
